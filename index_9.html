Presenter notes

Invisible to the room. Toggle with Shift+P. Never share screen with this open.

Open · first 60 seconds
Before I show you anything — you're probably expecting a lab rep to tell you your lab is bad. That's not this. I want to put a number on something you already feel, and you tell me if it's worth fixing.
Do: slow, downward inflection, hands open. Pause two full seconds after "worth fixing."
Accusation audit — naming their suspicion first removes the reason to hold it.
L1 · open the wound (scenes section)
Which one of these three is your Tuesday?
Walk me through the last seat that went sideways — what did that do to the rest of the day?
Do: point at the screen, not at them. Mirror their last 3 words, then silence 4+ seconds.
Narrative transportation + mirroring — their own story lowers counter-arguing; the mirror gets detail without interrogation.
L2 · quantify (calculator)
Let's not use my defaults — roughly how many C&B units a month? And of those, how many come back needing more than a quick adjustment?
What's an hour of chair time worth here — five hundred? Seven? (bracket high; they'll correct you)
Do: hand them the slider if in person. Read the big number out loud once, then stop talking.
Bracketing + endowment — numbers they enter are theirs, not a vendor's claim; the monthly line is loss framing (losses weigh ~2× gains).
Recap + L3 · worth fixing?
So if I've got this right: remakes are around [their %], every one eats a re-temp and a chair hour, and the front desk can't answer "is it in yet" without calling. Is that fair?
Is that a problem worth fixing this quarter — or is it livable for now?
Do: wait for "that's right," not "you're right." If you get "livable," label it: Sounds like the pain isn't bad enough to change anything yet.
"That's right" signals real agreement; consistency principle makes the yes here carry into the ask.
Show · case rail + compare
Here's where your Tuesday crown would be right now — and here's who can see it.
Do: let the rail animate through one full cycle without narrating. Then point at the two "pain" rows on the left column.
Show, don't describe — contrast effect makes the digital column feel faster than any stat would.
Objections · labels first
"We're happy with our lab."
It sounds like the relationship matters more than the turnaround — that's fair. This isn't a switch; it's one case next to theirs.
"We don't have a scanner."
Seems like that's the real blocker, not the lab. What would it take to get one case scanned? (then walk through the scanner path per current program terms)
"Not now."
What's the cost of waiting a quarter? — point at the monthly line.
Do: label, pause, then calibrated "what/how." Never "why."
Labeling defuses the emotion behind the objection; calibrated questions give them control while they solve it.
Objection · “Do you do All-on-X?”
Not the final arch — not today. I’d rather lose that case than have you find out on the one that matters.
What we do run end to end is the implant overdenture — CBCT, plan, guide, immediate denture, final locator. Plus guides, scan bodies, abutments, implant crowns and bridges.
If they push on timing: I’m not going to give you a date I can’t stand behind. When it’s real, you’ll hear it from me first.
If they ask “can I scan it and send it elsewhere?” For All-on-X and All-on-4 — yes. That’s the one carve-out, and it holds until we have the full-arch offering ourselves. Scan it here, send it wherever you send those today.
Do not over-extend it: the carve-out is All-on-X / All-on-4 only. Overdentures, crowns, bridges, partials, guides — those are Dandy cases on a Dandy scanner.
Do: say the “no” flat and unhurried — late-night-DJ voice, no rising inflection, no apology face. Then pause a full two seconds before the “what we do run” line. Do not rush to fill it.
Know the line: Practice Agreement §8 reads as blanket exclusivity — Dandy equipment orders Dandy cases. The All-on-X / All-on-4 allowance is the practical exception while the offering is being built, so state it as the exception it is, never as “your scans go anywhere.” Physical impressions to other labs are permitted under §8 regardless.
Volunteering the disqualifier is the strongest authority move on the page — a rep who names their own gap gets believed on everything else. It also removes the fear underneath the question: that saying yes to Dandy costs them their arch lab.
